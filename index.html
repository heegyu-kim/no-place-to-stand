Summary of the accident Section 1

Paul Virilio said that to invent the ship is to invent the shipwreck. Every technology brings its own accident. So what accident came with generative AI in architecture?

This accident does not fall down. It does not catch fire. The results are faster, more convincing, and more beautiful than before. So no one calls it an accident. This report records that quiet accident. Designs that never ask where the body will stand are piling up across the industry.

What each medium gave back, and what it lost Section 2

Each medium fixed one problem of the medium before it, and lost something else. VR is not an exception. VR brought the camera back to the height of the body. But it removed every sense except sight. And even the sight that remains is not the sight we have in real space.

Then one more thing is added: a label written by someone else. All of this happens in a few seconds, with no time to check.

The classes carry the names of feelings. Warm, cozy, calm. But these labels were given by someone who never entered the space. They looked at one photo on a screen. When you ask AI for a warm space, you do not get warmth. You get the average of photos labeled warm.

Marks and wounds: none. No body was present at the scene of the image. The figure above is an outline only.

The camera was at the height of a human eye. But the lens is 14 mm. The human eye cannot see a scene this wide in straight lines all at once. We turn our head, walk, and move our eyes, and we gather it little by little. When the eye looks up at the skylight, the vertical lines of the columns come together. In this image, the columns stay straight to the end.

A photo at least had a camera in that place once. A rendering does not even have that place. The light, the people, and the space are all calculated and placed. The most important thing in this image is the water. But there is no cold water, no echo of the pool, no wet floor. AI learns images like this as the facts of architecture.

To be more exact, AI did not even learn architecture with eyes. It learned from images that a lens stretched, a frame cut, and one eye saw without walking.

VR gave back the height of the body, but it erased the rest of the body. AI inherited all of these losses at once.

So an architect who designs with AI gets spaces that never asked where the body will stand. This accident leaves no wreckage. The building stands. The photo looks good. The only thing missing is the body that would stand inside it.

Investigator's note Section 5

The investigator who writes this report was at the scene. In architectural practice, I made images of spaces with no people. I made the vertical lines straight. With renderings and VR, I showed spaces before they were built. The rendering in Exhibit C is an image I made.

So this is not an accusation. It is a statement. I am one of the people who made these losses, long before AI. What AI did was inherit those losses, and let anyone make them, every moment, without end.

I hereby declare that I was present at the scene described herein, that I took part in making the images in question, and that the information contained in this report is true and correct to the best of my knowledge and belief.

Safety recommendation Section 6

The way to prevent this accident is not to throw AI away. It is to put the body and the image back in the right order.

Current orderImage, image, image, then bodyreverse this
Recommended orderBody, sound, trace, then image

In our next work, we build a space in the dark. We build first with sound and the body, not with the eyes. Only after the trace remains as a plan do we look at the image. Then the image does not replace the space. It becomes a record of where the body has been.
